"How much storage does ₹2 lakh buy?" is one of the most useful questions you can ask about a NAS. It's also a hard one to answer by hand, because the chassis, the drive size, the number of drives and the RAID level all trade off against each other.

So we worked it out properly. For each budget below, we took every NAS unit and drive on our price list, tried every combination that keeps your data safe from a drive failure, and picked the one that gives the most usable space.

Prices include GST and cover the NAS unit and drives. Installation and annual maintenance are extra. These figures are from our price list in September 2026 — prices move, and the [configurator](/nas-config) always uses today's.

## The results

- **₹62,000 — 2 TB.** Synology DS223J with two 2 TB drives, mirrored (RAID 1). The least a protected NAS can cost.
- **₹1,00,000 — 6 TB.** DS223J with two 6 TB drives, RAID 1.
- **₹1,50,000 — 12 TB.** QNAP TS-433-4G with four 4 TB drives, RAID 5.
- **₹2,00,000 — 20 TB.** TS-433-4G with three 10 TB drives, RAID 5 — and a free bay for a fourth drive later.
- **₹3,00,000 — 32 TB.** TS-433-4G with three 16 TB drives, RAID 5.
- **₹5,00,000 — 64 TB.** QNAP TS-664-8G with five 16 TB drives, RAID 5.
- **₹7,50,000 — 108 TB.** QNAP TS-832PX-4G with seven 18 TB drives, RAID 5.
- **₹10,00,000 — 144 TB.** TS-832PX-4G with seven 24 TB drives, RAID 5.
- **₹20,00,000 — 288 TB.** Two TS-832PX-4G units, each with seven 24 TB drives.

## What the numbers show

**The entry point is ₹62,000.** The cheapest unit we stock is ₹24,000 and the cheapest drive is ₹19,000, and any RAID level needs at least two drives. Below that, you're buying a single disk with no protection at all.

**Between ₹1 lakh and ₹1.5 lakh, the jump is big.** At ₹1 lakh you're limited to a 2-bay mirror, which uses half its raw space. At ₹1.5 lakh a 4-bay unit becomes affordable, and RAID 5 keeps 75% of the raw space. Usable storage doubles for half as much money again.

**Bigger drives cost less per terabyte.** A 2 TB drive costs about ₹9,500 per TB on our list; drives from 10 TB to 24 TB cost about ₹4,800–5,700 per TB. That's why the recommendations above lean towards fewer, larger drives.

**Free bays are worth having.** Several of these configurations leave a bay empty. That isn't waste — it's where next year's capacity goes, without replacing anything.
