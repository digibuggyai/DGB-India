Synology and QNAP make most of the small-business NAS units sold in India, and both make good ones. Their software is mature, their hardware is reliable, and either will serve an office well.

But they differ in ways that matter when you're choosing between them. Here are the differences we see when we specify systems for clients.

## Which drives you can use

This is the biggest difference in 2026, and the one most people don't know about.

Synology's 2025 Plus models — the DS225+, DS425+, DS725+, DS925+, DS1525+ and DS1825+ — launched with a policy of supporting only Synology's own drives. With DSM 7.3, Synology relaxed this: third-party drives can now be installed and used to build storage pools. But Synology still recommends drives from its own compatibility list, and few third-party drives have been certified for these models so far.

QNAP has no such policy. Its units work with the NAS and enterprise drives from Seagate and Western Digital that most suppliers stock.

If you're buying a 2025 Synology model, confirm which drives are supported before you order.

## How big each drive can be

The largest drive Synology sells is 24 TB, the HAT5320-24T, and Synology's own capacity figures assume 24 TB drives.

QNAP doesn't publish a single maximum. Its units support whatever is on each model's compatibility list, and today that includes 32 TB drives such as Seagate's IronWolf Pro — the largest NAS drives currently made.

In practice, an 8-bay QNAP unit can reach 256 TB of raw capacity with 32 TB drives, against 192 TB for an 8-bay Synology with 24 TB drives.

## Room to grow

Both brands let you add bays later with an expansion unit, but they do it differently:

- **Synology** uses its DX525 expansion unit. The DS725+, DS925+, DS1525+ and DS1825+ support it; the DS223J, DS225+ and DS425+ do not. The DS1825+ can grow from 8 bays to 18 with two expansion units.
- **QNAP** uses its TR and TL series enclosures, which connect by USB or SATA. The TS-462, TS-464, TS-664, TS-832PX and TS-873A all support them.

If you expect your storage to grow, check this before you buy — it's much cheaper than replacing the whole unit.

## Networking

Most units in both ranges ship with 2.5 GbE ports, which is plenty for a small office. For faster links, several QNAP units take a PCIe card for 10 GbE, and the TS-832PX has two 10 GbE SFP+ ports built in. On the Synology side, the DS1525+ takes an optional 10 GbE module, and the DS1825+ supports up to 25 GbE with an add-in card.

## Price

On our current price list, QNAP gives more usable storage for the money above about ₹1.5 lakh. That's partly the cheaper chassis at each bay count, and partly the larger drives it supports. At the very bottom of the range, the Synology DS223J and QNAP TS-233 cost the same.

## So which one?

Choose **Synology** if your team values its software — Synology's DSM is widely regarded as the more polished of the two — and you're happy to work with its drive policy.

Choose **QNAP** if you want the most capacity for your budget, the widest choice of drives, or 10 GbE networking on a mid-range unit.
